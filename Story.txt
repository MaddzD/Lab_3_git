The quiet didn’t drop all at once; it settled like dust over the shattered remains of the Interstate.

Mara adjusted the grip on her steel wrench, her breathing shallow behind a dirt-streaked bandana. Below the overpass, the highway was a river of rust and static—dozens of rusted sedans bumper-to-bumper, and between them, the wanderers.

They weren't the fast, screaming monsters from old midnight movies. These were relentless in their silence. Dragged feet. Jawbones clicking against dry throats. A slow, agonizing tide of grey skin and hollow eyes, driven by nothing more than the residual muscle memory of a world that had died three winters ago.

Beside her, Eli nudged a loose piece of concrete. It chipped off the edge, dropping sixty feet before landing with a sharp clack against a hood.

Down below, fifty heads snapped upward in eerie unison. No growls. Just the dry, synchronized rasp of a hundred heels turning on gravel.

Suddenly, a bright, melodic chime echoed over the ruined highway, followed by a squeaking public address speaker: "Attention shoppers! Flash sale in aisle four. Fifty percent off all canned goods."

The horde paused instantly. As one, the fifty infected turned away from the overpass and began marching in perfect, orderly lines toward the grand opening of a pristine, brightly lit Supercenter sitting at the edge of the clearing—completely undamaged, its doors sliding open automatically.

Mara blinked, stunned. Through the store's floor-to-ceiling glass windows, dozens of infected were calmly grabbing shopping carts, scanning empty air at self-checkout kiosks, and patiently queuing up.

Eli grabbed her arm, pointing toward the store's automatic doors. Standing behind the customer service counter, immaculate in a blue vest and wearing a wide, terrifyingly rigid smile, was a manager with sunken, grey skin.
